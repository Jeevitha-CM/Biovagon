Where Biovagon came from

NOT A PITCH DECK.
A FEELING.

Biovagon was not a pitch deck before it was a company. It was a feeling.

I kept watching scientists at every level — including myself — lose entire afternoons to bioinformatics workflows that should have taken thirty minutes. Five tabs, six logins, exported CSVs nobody could parse, and the constant refrain of "we should build a pipeline for that, someday." I got tired of waiting for someday. So I started building. That was 2024. It has not slowed down since.

The name came during my MTech. It stuck because it sounded like what I wanted the company to be: a small thing with everything important loaded inside, going somewhere on purpose.

How the company actually runs

ONE BRAIN,
FOUR JOBS.

Biovagon and the PhD are not separate jobs. They share a brain. Most of what is on the roadmap started as a problem in my own research. If a tool cannot survive my own bench, it does not ship to anyone else's.

Right now the company is one person doing the work of about four:

CEO Work
Product direction, pricing, brand, and operations.
Founder Work
Specs, code review, and shipping.
Public-Facing Work
Writing, community, and long-form thinking.
The PhD Work
The actual science that keeps all of it honest.

This stack is intentional, not a flex. It keeps the feedback loop tight. When something is broken, I find out within a day, because I am the one using it.

What is live, what is next

SHIPPING ONLY
WHEN IT'S READY.

Primerly is the flagship. Free integrated primer design, in your browser, no signup wall. The custom primer service is its paid counterpart for teams who would rather send a brief and get primers back than learn another platform.

A few more tools are already in the build queue. I am not naming them yet. They will show up here when they are ready to survive a real bench, and not a day before. One of them is going to make a lot of people in this field very happy, and a few people very nervous.

Biovagon will scale beyond a single founder eventually. That is the plan. But only when the foundation is the kind I would be willing to hand off without rewriting it first.

But it is also more breakable than it looks. I am out here breaking my piece of it from one laptop in Chennai, with a PhD to finish on the side. You can break your piece of it too.

If you are sitting on a biotech idea and the loudest voice in your head is "who am I to start a company" — that voice is wrong, very normal, and entirely temporary. I have not solved it either. I have just learned to ship anyway.

My DMs are open and I actually answer them. Not "we will get back to you in 5–7 business days" — I mean me, typing back. So if you want to ask about:

  • Whether to do a PhD, where to do one, and how to survive it
  • Starting a one-person biotech without funding

Message me. The worst that happens is I reply.
